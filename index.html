Your 8-Week Roadmap

Select a week to open its instructions. Opening another week closes the previous one.

Week 1Form Your Group and Understand the ScenarioClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Read the project guide and assessment criteria.
  • Select your department and group.
  • Record names, IDs, and preliminary roles.
  • Study your assigned scenario and required negative test.

What you must provide

  • Group number and members
  • Scenario and role allocation
  • Short problem interpretation
  • Initial security properties
Week 2Analyze Assets, Threats, and RequirementsClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Identify users, protected assets, attackers, and trust boundaries.
  • List realistic threats, assumptions, and exclusions.
  • Write at least five testable security requirements.

What you must provide

  • Checkpoint 1: 1–2 page analysis
  • Assets, actors, boundaries, threats
  • Approved / Correct / Resubmit
Week 3Design the Architecture and CryptographyClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Draw components and protected data flow.
  • Select algorithms, protocols, and maintained libraries.
  • Document key ownership and lifecycle.
  • Explain nonce, IV, salt, timestamp, or identifier handling.

What you must provide

  • Checkpoint 2: architecture diagram
  • Mechanism and key-management table
  • Preliminary test plan
  • Updated task allocation
Week 4Build the Core End-to-End PrototypeClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Accept valid input.
  • Apply the security operation.
  • Produce protected output.
  • Process it at the receiver or server.
  • Show successful verification.

What you must provide

  • Checkpoint 3: running code
  • Repository/version evidence
  • Known issues and member progress
Week 5Complete the Required ControlsClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Complete authentication and authorization where relevant.
  • Complete encryption, hashing, signatures, replay protection, validation, logging, and safe errors.
  • Check that secrets are not hard-coded or logged.

What you must provide

  • System approximately 70–80% complete
  • All required features present
  • Only sample test data and authorized systems
Week 6Test Security and the Failure CaseClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Test the valid workflow.
  • Test modified data, wrong key/user/role, and malformed input.
  • Perform the assigned negative test.
  • Record expected and actual results.

What you must provide

  • Checkpoint 4: successful test
  • Required negative test
  • Labeled evidence and pass/fail result
Week 7Finalize, Record, Submit, and FreezeClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Finish report, source, README, tests, slides, and video.
  • Complete contribution and AI/tool declarations.
  • Rehearse the live demonstration and viva.

What you must provide

  • Submit 48–72 hours before the exam
  • 8–12 minute final video
  • Submitted version is frozen
Week 8Practical Exam and Individual VivaClick to view activities and required output⌄

What you must do

  • Open the submitted project and sample test data.
  • Give a 2-minute introduction.
  • Show the successful workflow and required negative test.
  • Answer group and individual questions.

What you must provide

  • Successful live workflow: 4 min
  • Negative test: 3 min
  • Individual viva: about 2 min each

Rules and Safety

You must

  • Use sample test data with no real personal, financial, medical, academic, or confidential information.
  • Work only on systems owned by your group or approved by the teaching team.
  • Use reputable libraries and cite external material.
  • Understand and test every submitted component.
  • Declare permitted AI/tool assistance.

You must not

  • Attack or scan real third-party systems.
  • Use real records, credentials, examination files, or confidential documents.
  • Invent a cryptographic algorithm.
  • Submit a downloaded complete project.
  • Copy another group’s work.
